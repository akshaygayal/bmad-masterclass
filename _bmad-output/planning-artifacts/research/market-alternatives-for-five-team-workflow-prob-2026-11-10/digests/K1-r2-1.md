## Rovo readiness checker teardown

- **What it is:** An out-of-the-box Rovo agent in Jira (claim 1). It is available "as soon as you have access to Rovo". Plan tiers and pricing were not verified (claim 6).
- **Inputs:** Jira work items: description, required fields, acceptance criteria, estimate, links. It can also read, edit and create Jira/Confluence content (claims 2, 3).
- **Criteria:** Atlassian's pages are inconsistent. The product page names four (Completeness, Clarity, Auditability, Estimated), the docs say "team's definition of ready", and the lesson lists field/AC/estimate/link checks. How a team configures its DoR is not documented in the pages read. Duplicating agents with custom knowledge sources is documented generally, not for this agent specifically (claim 4).
- **Output:** Per-criterion emoji scores plus improvement suggestions (product page), or an overall Pass/Warnings/Fail that automation can write to a custom field (lesson) (claims 2, 3).
- **Triggering:** On demand in chat (inferred from example prompts), or through a Jira Automation "Use Agent" action, for example a scheduled JQL sweep before sprint planning or a transition/creation trigger (claims 3, 7).
- **Documented limitations:** None stated by Atlassian (claim 6).
- **Multi-perspective / role-based:** None documented. It judges the text of the ticket against a checklist. It does not gather or compare different people's interpretations (claim 5).
- **Label caveat:** All of this is documented capability. No independent evaluation of its effectiveness was found, and I did not observe it directly.

## Substitute map

| Substitute | What it compares | Gap against the K1 mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rovo Readiness Checker (and similar AI ticket checkers: Refinely, StoryPilot, StoryCraft) | Ticket text against a DoR checklist | Looks at one artifact, not interpretations held by people. Cannot detect a ticket that "looks complete" but is understood differently. | claims 1–5, 10 |
| Planning poker (Agile Toolbox etc.) | Independent size estimates, revealed together | Same independent-then-reveal structure, but only on size. Divergence is a proxy signal for differing understanding. | claim 9 |
| Example Mapping / Three Amigos (practice; Example Mapping for Jira, TeamUp Labs) | Rules, examples and questions built together | Collaborative rather than independent, so anchoring on the first speaker is possible. Has some case-study evidence of improving shared understanding. | claims 10, 15 |
| Async consensus collection (Estimation & Priority Hub) | Team members' inputs before consensus | Snippet suggests individual inputs are collected; unclear whether these are interpretations of behaviour/edge cases. | claim 10 |
| Perspective-Based Reading (inspection technique) | Defects found by readers taking different roles | Defect detection, not interpretation divergence. Meta-analysis shows no clear benefit over checklists. | claims 13, 14 |
| Ambiguity research (Yang 2011, Kaiya 2005, Ferrari 2014) | Divergence across readers or stakeholders | Closest conceptual match. Research prototypes, not shipped products (as far as found). | claim 12 |

## Leads

- Yang, de Roeck, Gervasi, Willis, Nuseibeh 2011 (RE journal): the "nocuous ambiguity" approach is a ready-made way to operationalise "interpretation mismatch". Follow its citations forward.
- Kaiya et al. 2005 AGORA: discordance detection among stakeholders.
- Utrecht RE'21 Example Mapping paper and its Refinement Evaluation Tool: a possible instrument for measuring shared understanding in a validation experiment.
- Ciolkowski 2009 full paper and the Madeyski & Kitchenham re-analysis: student vs professional moderators.
- Later NaPiRE rounds (2018+ data) and Wagner et al. "Status quo in requirements engineering" (TOSEM 2019). These come from the same initiative, so they are not independent.
