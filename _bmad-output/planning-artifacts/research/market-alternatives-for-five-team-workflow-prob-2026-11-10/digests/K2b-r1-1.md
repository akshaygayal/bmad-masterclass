## Substitute map
- **Dedicated products:** HR/performance suites with goal modules that offer optional cascading and alignment trees (Lattice, claims 1–3); OKR-specific tools that advocate alignment over cascading (Tability, claim 11; BetterWorks, claim 9). They target the company → team → individual hierarchy, and admin settings control how strict it is (claims 1–2).
- **Platform/embedded features:** Microsoft Viva Goals was the main embedded M365/Teams OKR offering and was retired on 2025-12-31 with no named successor (claims 4–5). That leaves a possible gap in embedded tooling, but no demand data supports it.
- **Non-software practices:** transparent, public goals instead of cascading, plus FAST goals (claim 6); "aligned, not cascaded" bottom-up setting against a short list of company priorities (claim 11); fixing communication and decision-making before adopting OKRs (claim 10).
- **Services:** OKR consultancies and coaches (ZOKRI, Stacey Barr, claim 11). Their advisory content is partly a sales channel.
- **Do-nothing:** dropping OKRs, or treating them as box-ticking (claims 10, 12). Spreadsheet or slide export is the fallback that Viva Goals' own migration path names (claim 4).

## Leads worth chasing
- Kim, Kim & Kwon 2020 (*Sustainability*, MDPI, open access): read in full for its method and its alignment → clarity → performance path.
- Pritchard, Harrell, DiazGranados & Guzman 2008 (ProMES meta-analysis, J. Applied Psychology): the strongest outcome evidence on goals plus feedback, though not about cascading.
- The Microsoft Viva Goals retirement FAQ, and Ally.io acquisition coverage, to date the product's lifespan.
- Goal modules in Workday, SAP SuccessFactors and BambooHR (cascading docs, plus "common/shared goals" pushed to many employees).
- Felipe Castro and Christina Wodtke (OKR practitioner originators) on cascading; John Cutler's "zombie practices" essay (The Beautiful Mess).
