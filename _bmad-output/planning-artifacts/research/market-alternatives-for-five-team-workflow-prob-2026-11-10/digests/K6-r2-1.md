## Regulatory scope summary (not legal advice)

- **NYC LL144:** Official DCWP guidance covers computer-based tools that use ML, statistical modelling, data analytics, or AI to produce a prediction or classification and that substantially assist or replace discretionary decision-making in hiring or promotion decisions "in the city". A non-AI form that requires interviewers to cite evidence does not, on the FAQ's wording, appear to generate a prediction or classification (Inference). Coverage of any AI feature, such as auto-summaries or suggested ratings, depends on facts: whether it outputs a score, tag, or ranking, and how heavily decision-makers rely on it. The verbatim statute (§20-870, including any exclusions) and the rule definitions ("simplified output"; the three "substantially assist" prongs) were not retrieved this run.
- **EU AI Act:** Amended by the AI Omnibus, which is in force from 27 Jul 2026 (official Commission page). Annex III high-risk obligations now apply from 2 Dec 2027. A purely non-AI tool would fall outside the Art. 3(1) definition if it involves no machine-based inference, but that depends on implementation. This is an unverified belief: official text was not retrieved. Any AI component used to evaluate candidates could fall under Annex III point 4 unless Art. 6(3) applies. That depends on facts, and profiling defeats the exception (unverified this run). Non-AI tools may still face other obligations, such as GDPR and employment or equality law.
- **GDPR:** Not verified this run. It is an unverified belief that interview notes are personal data, are subject to Art. 13/14 notices and Art. 15 access requests, and that Art. 22 applies only to decisions based solely on automated processing with legal or similarly significant effects. A human-made recommendation would not meet that test. An evidence-linked feedback record would likely increase what a candidate could obtain through an access request (Inference). Confirm against EUR-Lex 2016/679.
